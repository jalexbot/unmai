**The promise:** DMK's 2021 manifesto committed to abolishing NEET for Tamil Nadu medical admissions and restoring Class 12 board mark-based selection.

**What happened:**

On 13 September 2021, the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly unanimously passed the Admission to Undergraduate Medical Degree Courses Bill, 2021 — commonly called the Anti-NEET Bill. The bill sought to exempt Tamil Nadu from the national NEET examination and allow admissions based on Class 12 marks.

The Governor referred the bill to the President of India, as medical education falls under the Concurrent List of the Constitution (List III), requiring central assent.

As of October 2024, the President has not given assent to the bill. NEET remains mandatory for medical admissions in Tamil Nadu.

**Verdict: Partial.**
The DMK government did legislate to fulfil this promise — a significant step. However, implementation is blocked by the requirement for Presidential/central assent, which has not been granted. Students in Tamil Nadu continue to take NEET.

**Context:**
This is an example where a state government's ability to deliver on a promise is constrained by the Constitution's division of powers between state and centre. The DMK's promise was made without clarifying this constitutional constraint to voters.
